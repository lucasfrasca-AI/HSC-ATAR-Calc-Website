    How the ATAR is put together

    Each course's HSC mark is the average of the school assessment mark and the exam mark. UAC scales each course, adds up the best 10 units (always including the best 2 units of English) and ranks every student in the state on that aggregate.

    A third of the points can still be close to the middle of the state.

    ATAR is a rank, not a score: the percentage of the Year 12 age group finished above. Marks scale down hard below each course's mean, so a modest share of the 500 points can still land mid-pack.

    What to trust and what not to

    Eligibility rules checked +
    At least 10 ATAR units, including 2 units of English, at least 8 units of Category A courses, at least three courses of 2 units or more, and at least four subjects. No more than 2 units of Category B can count.
    Scaling estimates +
    Courses marked "estimated" use a generic scaling tier, not UAC data. They are there so every course produces a number, not so the number is exact. Open "Course settings" on a subject to enter real anchors from the latest UAC Scaling Report.
    The ATAR curve +
    A normal distribution fitted through two UAC reference points: aggregate 154.50 → ATAR 48.75 and aggregate 283.53 → ATAR 74.50. It is least accurate at the extremes.
    Moderation +
    In the real HSC your assessment mark is replaced by the exam mark sitting at your rank in your school's cohort. The optional estimate places your rank on an assumed cohort distribution. It is a rough guide — the real result depends on how your classmates actually perform.
